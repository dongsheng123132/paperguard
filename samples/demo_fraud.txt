A Pilot Study on Adolescent Math Anxiety and Test Scores

Abstract
We surveyed 24 high-school students using a 1-7 Likert scale on math anxiety and
related their scores to final exam outcomes. The reported mean anxiety score was
4.27 (SD = 1.10, N = 24). A treatment group of 17 students underwent a mindfulness
intervention and reported a post-treatment mean of 3.83 (SD = 0.91, N = 17).

Methods
All 24 students completed a 10-item anxiety inventory scored 1-7 per item. Final
exam scores were collected from the school registrar.

Results
Math anxiety was significantly correlated with exam scores, r(22) = 0.34, p = 0.001.
The intervention significantly reduced anxiety, t(16) = 2.05, p = 0.012.
A one-way ANOVA confirmed group differences, F(2, 21) = 1.20, p < 0.01.
A chi-square test of independence on coping style yielded chi2(3) = 7.81, p = 0.001.
Pearson correlation between anxiety and exam scores in subgroup A was r(15) = 0.92, p = 0.300.

Subgroup descriptive table:
Group A scores: 4.21, 5.33, 3.18, 4.27, 5.10, 3.92, 4.55, 5.01.
Group B scores: 4.21, 5.33, 3.18, 4.27, 5.10, 3.92, 4.55, 5.01.

Discussion
These findings broadly align with prior literature on math anxiety. We note that
the intervention effect, while statistically significant, may reflect ceiling
artifacts in the inventory.
